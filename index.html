The problem

"Connects up to 45 strands" and other box lies.

Every November the same two surprises: the strand count on the box is only half the rule (wattage gets a vote), and the circuit running your display was never told about your display. Strandmath turns your footage into strands, plug-in runs, circuit load and season cost - before the ladder comes out.

Plan the display

What it computes

Real strand count
Roofline, windows, rails and trees into lit feet, then strands - rounded up, because half a strand is not a thing.
Plug-in runs
The stricter of the box's strand limit and the common 216-watt run rule. This is the number of outlets you actually need.
The circuit check
Your display plus whatever else lives on that breaker, against the published 80% continuous-load rule - 1,440 W on a 15 A circuit.
Season cost
kWh and dollars at your rate, with the LED-vs-incandescent comparison on the same footage.

Why the box misleads

  • Two limits, one number printed. "Connects 45 strands" assumes the strand limit is what binds. For incandescent strings the 216-watt run rule usually binds first - sometimes at 2 strands.
  • The breaker was already busy. A garage freezer or a space heater on the same circuit turns a fine display into a tripped breaker on the coldest night of the year.
  • Watts hide in plain sight. 25 classic C9 bulbs draw about 175 watts - more than some space heaters on low. The same outline in LED draws 8.
